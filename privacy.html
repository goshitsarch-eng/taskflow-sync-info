TaskFlow privacy policy

Effective date: 30 September 2026

TaskFlow is a personal, local-first desktop task manager. Your tasks, descriptions, projects, comments, reminders, attachments, preferences, and activity history are stored on your computer. Cloud Sync is optional.

Google account and Drive access

When you choose Sign in with Google, TaskFlow requests your Google account identity and email address to identify your personal workspace and display the connected account. It also requests access to its own private application data in Google Drive (drive.appdata). This permission does not grant TaskFlow access to ordinary files in your Drive.

TaskFlow uploads and downloads your shared content and preferences through Google's APIs so that your signed-in computers can synchronize. This includes attachments, signed change history, device membership records, and recovered text versions. Local window geometry, startup registration, shortcuts, permissions, reminder delivery state, backup files, and credentials are not uploaded as workspace content.

TaskFlow does not operate a separate cloud server, sell your Google data, use it for advertising, or send it to an AI service. Google processes data sent to Google APIs under its own terms and privacy policy. Optional LAN Sync exchanges shared content with devices you explicitly pair.

Credentials and retention

Google refresh credentials are stored locally using the operating system's credential protection when available. If secure storage is unavailable, TaskFlow supports sign-in for the current session only. Refresh tokens are not included in synced content or shared with other devices.

Cloud Sync retains change history and recovered versions to support offline devices and recovery. Cloud and local history grow as you use the app. Deleting an item in TaskFlow synchronizes its deletion, but does not erase all retained historical versions.

Disconnecting Google Drive removes TaskFlow's saved Google credentials on that computer and keeps local content and queued edits. It does not delete private Drive data or disconnect other computers. You can revoke TaskFlow's access in your Google Account settings. Revocation stops further authorized access; it does not itself delete existing local copies or cloud history.

Limited use and contact

TaskFlow's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
